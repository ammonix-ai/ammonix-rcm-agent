Ammonix vs the agentic approach

Four decision systems worked the same 300 simulated reimbursement claims with identical payer randomness. Ammonix v0.3 wins on quality and real collections at zero decision-time LLM cost. The LLM agent is the cleanest rule-follower but surrenders the most claims; v0.2’s raw-dollar lead was duplicate payments, not real money.

How to read this honestly
  • All four arms played the same 300 fresh claims (tranche 92, indices 4000–4299, never seen in any training data) with identical payer randomness — the v0.3 arm proved episode identity with a bit-exact personas-replay canary.
  • “Capped” collections count at most each claim’s allowed amount; anything above is a duplicate payment the scoring ignores and a real payer would claw back.
  • Escalated touches fall back to the same human (persona) policy in every arm. The agentic arm runs the same pinned Qwen 27B that powers Ammonix’s writing layer, so the comparison measures architecture, not model quality.
  • One draw of 300 episodes: the v0.3 vs agentic success-rate gap (1 pt) is within noise; the score gap, mistake gaps, and the duplicate-payment decomposition are solid.
  • Ammonix v0.3 is built and green but awaiting its release sign-off (GATE-P10); v0.2 is the shipped state. Source: Agentic_Baseline/runs/reports/comparison.json.
